AI-Native Academia: Authorship, Peer Review, and Conference Governance under AI

Once AI errors enter published papers, they don't come back out. This workshop names the failure mode (human-AI co-hallucination) and brings together the PC chairs already designing the response.

Call for Papers Important Dates
01

Overview

Scholarly publication is a chain: drafting, citation, submission, review, rebuttal, meta-review, acceptance, indexing, corpus reuse for the next model. AI is now active at every node, and an error at one node propagates through every downstream one, with no undo button.

We call the failure mode human-AI co-hallucination: false or unsupported scholarly claims that no model and no human would produce alone, but that arise through their interaction and become institutionalized through trust, time pressure, and publication incentives.

Program chairs at major venues are already running live interventions on the publication system: ICML 2026's Policy A/B reviewer test and self-ranking deployment, ICLR 2026's venue-side LLM-detector pipeline, Stanford's Agents4Science 2025 full-loop pilot, NeurIPS 2025's Responsible Reviewing Initiative. These prototypes are scattered across different chairs with no shared formulation. This workshop is the convergence point.

Call for Papers

We invite technical, empirical, and policy submissions on eight topics that decompose the publication pipeline by actor, artifact, intervention type, and timescale.

1 Human-AI Co-Hallucination & Error Propagationcross-cutting Taxonomies of failure paths; observable signals that distinguish co-hallucination from pure model or pure human error; models of how local AI errors become institutionalized through trust, time pressure, and publication incentives.
2 AI-Assisted Authorship & Rebuttaldrafting + rebuttal LLM use in drafting, related-work synthesis, citation selection, code and figure generation, polishing, rebuttal; disclosure thresholds, accessibility for non-native writers, the boundary between assistance and distorted communication.
3 AI-Native Peer Review & Decision Supportreviewer / AC / PC Human-only vs. AI-only vs. collaborative reviewing; review correctness, calibration, fairness, decision impact; identification of AI-written reviews; mechanism design under AI pressure including the ICML 2026 Isotonic-Mechanism deployment.
4 Manuscripts as Attack Surfacessubmission, adversarial Hidden prompt injection in PDFs and LaTeX, adversarial figures and metadata, jailbreaks against review assistants, document sanitization, review-tool security, red-teaming of AI-assisted conference workflows.
5 Citation & Credit Governancecitation node Citation hallucination, support-of-claim verification, citation manipulation, citation-recommendation bias, missing-credit detection, bibliometric distortion, knowledge-graph contamination.
6 Provenance, Plagiarism, Authorship Integritysubmission + post-pub AI-generated paper detection extending the ICLR/ICML 2026 audits; watermarking; text and figure provenance; copyright and memorization; due process; harms of false-positive detection on honest non-native authors.
7 Recursive Scholarly Feedback Loopscorpus reuse; long-horizon Synthetic-data contamination and model collapse in scientific text; cognitive decline from low-quality training text; benchmark leakage; AI-generated review norms; long-term degradation or homogenization of scientific knowledge.
8 Publication Infrastructure & Policygovernance Enforceable AI-use policies for authors, reviewers, ACs, PCs, and platforms; audit trails for AI-influenced decisions; OpenReview, arXiv, ACM, IEEE infrastructure; scaling review from 15,000 to 30,000+ submissions without collapse into AI-only review.

Submission tracks (via OpenReview, non-archival): Short papers (4 pages) and long papers (9 pages), references excluded. Work already accepted at NeurIPS main or other ML venues is excluded.
